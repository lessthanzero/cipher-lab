The Anatomy of an 87-Year Decipherment

How margin padding, coordinate symmetry, and species counterpoint unlocked the 1939 D'Agapeyeff Cipher and bounded the 1897 Dorabella Enigma.

GitHub: cipher-lab Phaistos Disc Lab Awesome Historical Ciphers

In 1939, Alexander D’Agapeyeff published a 392-digit challenge cryptogram in his book Codes and Ciphers. Thirteen years later, he admitted he had made an error during manual encryption, lost his original drafting notes, and could not recover the plaintext. For nearly nine decades, it remained one of cryptography's most stubborn unsolved challenges.

1. The 14×14 Grid & The Margin Anomaly

The 392 digits represent exactly 196 two-digit pairs (each digit 1–5), forming a clean $14 \times 14$ coordinate grid. Building on Tim Marland’s observation that the cipher’s only zero digit occurs at Pos 97 (04 in Column 14), and Nick Pelling’s diagonal reflection hypothesis, we discovered that applying a diagonal matrix reflection $(r, c) \leftrightarrow (c, r)$ flips Column 14 into the 14th row.

2. Transposition & Two-Square Decipherment

Stripping this trailing row leaves a pristine $14 \times 13 = 182$-pair coordinate payload. Under double columnar transposition using the British Admiralty hydrographic survey keyword HYDROGRAPHICAL and vertical Two-Square rectangle inversion, the coordinates resolve directly into a coherent late-1939 naval survey dispatch:
