### Option B: Single Long-Form Post (X Premium)

```text
In my spare time, I (+my agents!) built an interactive research workbench for the 3,700-year-old Phaistos Disc.

Instead of treating it purely as an undeciphered text puzzle, I looked at it through an interaction design lens:

1. Ergonomics: A 16 cm clay disc fits naturally between two hands. Stamping 241 signs using bespoke carved stone/metal punches required ~a week of skilled artisan labor (vs 2 hours to write by hand). You only invest in tooling for recurring, high-stakes ceremonies.

2. The Spiral "Teleprompter": The spiral isn't decorative—it's continuous information architecture. It eliminates line-break tracking errors when a priest is chanting and walking during a ritual.

3. Ritual Architecture: Not all signs are equal letters. Structural clustering suggests ceremonial cues: offering sequences (ship models, agricultural yield), musical pacing marks, and divine addresses. The Hagia Triada sarcophagus frescoes show this exact liturgical workflow.

I wanted to test the physical interaction, so we modeled rotation speeds, Monte Carlo sign null-controls, and synthetic Bronze Age lyre/aulos audio.
```
