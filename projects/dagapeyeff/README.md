# D’Agapeyeff Cipher Project

- **Origin**: Alexander D’Agapeyeff, *Codes and Ciphers* (1939), p. 158.
- **Corpus**: 392 digits (196 pairs), restricted entirely to digits `1` through `5`.
- **Digit Frequencies**:
  - `1`: 78 (19.9%)
  - `2`: 78 (19.9%)
  - `3`: 79 (20.2%)
  - `4`: 78 (19.9%)
  - `5`: 79 (20.2%)
- **Distribution**: Almost perfectly uniform across digits 1–5, strongly indicative of a $5 \times 5$ Polybius square fractionation step (such as Nihilist, Bifid, or ADFGX without the final letter substitution).
- **Core Challenge**: D’Agapeyeff publicly admitted in 1952 that he had forgotten the exact algorithm and suspected an encipherment clerical error had corrupted the ciphertext.
- **Research Method**: Fractionated transposition hypothesis space combined with bounded error-slip operators and strict negative-twin controls.
